Long videos often contain several useful short-form moments. The slow part is finding them, choosing clean start and end points, reframing horizontal footage, correcting captions, and preparing each clip for publishing.

AI video clipping tools reduce that work, but they do not all solve the same problem. Some prioritize automatic clip discovery. Some give editors more control. Others combine recording, clipping, and publishing, while MintiStudio also offers a done-for-you path for creators who do not want to run every production step themselves.

This guide compares six relevant options for someone asking: **I already create long videos. Which tool should I use to turn them into short clips?**

The comparison is based on current product pages and official documentation reviewed on November 6, 2026. It is a feature and workflow comparison, not a claim that we hands-on tested every product. Features and plan availability can change, so confirm any must-have capability before committing your workflow.

There is no honest universal winner. The best choice depends on where your current bottleneck sits.

## 1. OpusClip: best for broad AI clipping automation

[OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/) combines automatic clip discovery with virality scoring, animated captions, AI reframing, a browser editor, and social scheduling. Its current ClipAnything workflow is positioned for a wider range of footage than speech-only podcasts, including vlogs, gaming, sports, interviews, and explainers.

### Strengths

- AI finds and ranks moments from long footage.
- Captions and multiple aspect ratios are part of the same editing flow.
- The editor supports manual refinement after generation.
- Clips can move into OpusClip's social calendar without a separate export-and-upload step.
- A free plan provides a practical way to evaluate the workflow.

### Limitations

OpusClip's breadth can be more than a creator needs if the only job is turning straightforward talking-head videos into a few Shorts. Virality scores help prioritize a review queue, but they are still predictions rather than a guarantee that a clip will perform. You should review context, cuts, names, and captions before publishing.

### Who should choose it

Choose OpusClip if you work with varied video formats and want discovery, editing, reframing, and scheduling inside one self-serve product.

## 2. Klap: best for a focused long-video-to-short workflow

[Klap](https://klap.app/how-it-works) turns an uploaded video or supported link into ranked vertical clips. Its documented workflow includes transcript-based moment selection, a virality score, active-speaker reframing, editable animated captions, browser editing, and a publishing calendar.

### Strengths

- The workflow stays focused on turning long videos into ready-to-post clips.
- Clip scores include an explanation, which can make the first review pass faster.
- Reframing supports active speakers and layouts for multi-person or screen-based footage.
- Captions, cuts, framing, branding, and scheduling live in the same product.
- A free trial is available for evaluating a source video.

### Limitations

Klap is still a self-serve workflow: someone needs to review the selected moments, correct anything that is wrong, and decide what should actually be published. As with any score, a high ranking does not replace editorial judgment about your audience or message.

### Who should choose it

Choose Klap if you want a direct path from a long, speech-led video to a ranked batch of captioned clips and a social calendar.

## 3. Vizard: best for content teams using a social calendar

[Vizard](https://vizard.ai/) combines AI-generated clips with a full browser editor, automatic reframing, captions, workspace features, and social scheduling. Its documentation also describes an auto-schedule flow that can select generated clips and place them into a publishing plan.

### Strengths

- AI clip discovery and editing are available in one workspace.
- The editor supports caption, layout, and branding adjustments.
- A calendar supports scheduling to connected social accounts.
- Shared workspace and team features are available for collaborative production.
- A free plan includes AI-generated clips and editor access.

### Limitations

The combination of credits, workspaces, editing, and publishing options means teams should check the current plan details against their volume and collaboration needs. Auto-scheduling can reduce manual posting, but teams should still define an approval step so generated clips do not bypass brand or accuracy review.

### Who should choose it

Choose Vizard if several people need to move clips through editing, review, and a shared publishing calendar.

## 4. Riverside: best for podcast recording and repurposing

[Riverside Magic Clips](https://riverside.fm/magic-clips) sits inside a broader recording and editing platform. It identifies highlights from a recording, creates clips, and lets the user adjust duration, speaker focus, aspect ratio, layout, branding, and captions.

### Strengths

- Recording, transcription, editing, and clipping can stay in one environment.
- Magic Clips finds highlights automatically.
- Text-based editing is useful for interviews and podcasts.
- Users can adjust layouts, ratios, speakers, captions, and branding.
- Riverside offers a free plan that includes Magic Clips.

### Limitations

Riverside makes the most sense when its recording workflow is also valuable to you. If your long-form library already lives elsewhere and you only want a specialized clipping-and-scheduling tool, a clipping-first product may be a more direct fit. Its official workflow emphasizes export and sharing rather than a dedicated multi-platform short-form scheduling calendar.

### Who should choose it

Choose Riverside if you record remote podcasts, interviews, or webinars and want repurposing to begin in the same project as the original recording.

## 5. Descript: best for text-based editing control

[Descript](https://www.descript.com/templates/create-clips) treats a transcript as the editing surface. Its Create Clips workflow can identify highlights, create clips in different aspect ratios, apply captions and layouts, and leave every result editable in the main Descript project.

### Strengths

- Video can be edited by changing text rather than working only on a timeline.
- AI can find highlights and create multiple social clips.
- Captions, layouts, aspect ratios, filler-word removal, and detailed edits remain adjustable.
- It works naturally for podcasts, webinars, interviews, and presentations.
- A free plan lets users try text-based editing and limited AI repurposing.

### Limitations

Descript is an editor first, so it suits people willing to make editorial decisions. Its official YouTube export documentation states that direct YouTube Shorts publishing is not supported; Shorts need to be downloaded and uploaded manually. If a built-in multi-platform short-form calendar is essential, consider a scheduling-focused tool.

### Who should choose it

Choose Descript if transcript-level control and broader audio/video editing matter more than a fully automated clip-to-social workflow.

## How to choose an AI video clipping tool

### Decide how much editing control you want

If you want transcript-level control over every cut, Descript is a strong starting point. If you prefer to review a batch of automatically selected clips, OpusClip, Klap, Vizard, Riverside, or MintiStudio can shorten the discovery step.

### Check what your source library looks like

Creators with varied footage such as gaming, sports, or vlogs should examine OpusClip's broader visual clipping workflow. Podcasters who also need recording should consider Riverside. Creators with an existing library of speech-led YouTube videos may find MintiStudio's channel-based workflow more direct.

### Separate clip discovery from clip quality

An AI can rank a moment and still miss context, pronounce a name incorrectly in captions, or choose a weak ending. Look for a workflow that makes review and correction easy. A “virality score” is a sorting aid, not a performance promise.

### Decide whether publishing belongs in the same product

OpusClip, Klap, Vizard, and MintiStudio document built-in publishing or scheduling workflows. Riverside emphasizes export and sharing. Descript can publish some formats directly, but its official documentation says YouTube Shorts require manual upload.

### Choose between DIY and done-for-you

Most products in this comparison are self-serve tools. They reduce editing work, but you still operate the workflow. MintiStudio Autopilot is the option here for users who want the production steps prepared for them while retaining review before publishing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an AI video clipping tool?

An AI video clipping tool analyzes a longer video and helps turn selected moments into shorter clips. Depending on the product, it may also add captions, reframe horizontal footage for vertical viewing, score suggested moments, provide an editor, or schedule finished clips.

### Can AI turn long videos into Shorts?

Yes. The tools compared here can identify or create short clips from longer footage and format them for vertical viewing. The output should still be reviewed for context, caption accuracy, framing, and clean start and end points.

### What is the best AI tool for YouTube Shorts?

It depends on the workflow. OpusClip and Klap suit automated clip discovery, Vizard suits team and calendar workflows, Riverside suits podcast recording, Descript suits detailed text-based editing, and MintiStudio suits creators who want a choice between DIY and done-for-you production.

### Are AI clipping tools free?

Every product in this comparison offers either a free plan, free trial, or free tool. The included features and limits differ and can change, so check the product's current plan page before relying on a specific export quality, processing allowance, or publishing feature.

### Can AI find the best moments in a video?

AI can rank moments using signals such as topic, clarity, hooks, speaker activity, and whether a section works on its own. “Best” remains subjective. The creator still knows which claims are accurate, which story fits the audience, and which moment supports the wider content strategy.

### Do AI video clipping tools add captions automatically?

Yes. All six products in this comparison document automatic caption or transcription workflows. Caption text should be checked, especially for names, technical terms, accents, and noisy recordings.
